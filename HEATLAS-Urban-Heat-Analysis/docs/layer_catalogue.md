# GEE Layer Catalogue

This catalogue describes the map layers and grid-level variables currently generated by [`GEE/Jaipur_Heat_v3.js`](../GEE/Jaipur_Heat_v3.js). It is an inventory of the supplied script, not a list of planned platform features. Layer names below follow the labels used in the Google Earth Engine Code Editor wherever a `Map.addLayer()` call is present.

For the scientific definitions, formulas and modelling choices, see [`methodology.md`](methodology.md). The input collection identifiers and temporal filters are listed in [`data_sources.md`](data_sources.md).

## 1. How to read this catalogue

The script produces two related types of output:

- **Map layers:** images or feature collections added to the GEE map for visual inspection with `Map.addLayer()`.
- **Grid-level dataset:** a 34-band image stack summarised over the Jaipur 1 km grid using `reduceRegions()` and `ee.Reducer.mean()`.

A layer being visible on the map does not necessarily mean it is included in the final grid-level dataset. The differences are listed in Sections 8 and 9.

## 2. Study-area and grid layers

| GEE display name | Source / variable | What it represents | Included in final grid stack? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Jaipur Boundary` | FAO GAUL 2015 Level 2; filtered to `ADM2_NAME = Jaipur` | Administrative boundary used to clip and analyse the pilot study area. | No; used as the study-area boundary. |
| `Grid` | `gridFC`; `coveringGrid()` using EPSG:3857 and 1,000 m cells | Nominal 1 km reporting grid. Each feature is assigned a grid ID, administrative labels, area, centroid coordinates, run-date field and quality-status field. | The grid is the collection over which the final image stack is summarised. |
| `Heat Zones` | `heatZones`, derived from grid-level mean daytime LST | Grid features receive a temperature value and a text class: Extreme (>45 °C), High (>40 °C), Moderate (>35 °C), or Low (otherwise). The map layer is styled with blue outlines; the class is stored as a feature property. | No; maintained as a separate FeatureCollection. |
| `COMPLETE HEAT DATASET` | `dataset`, produced by `allBands.reduceRegions()` | Displays the grid features carrying the mean values of the stacked bands. The current visual style draws red outlines. | This is the display of the final grid-level FeatureCollection itself. |

## 3. Thermal and heat-risk layers

| GEE display name | Band / variable | What it represents | Included in final grid stack? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `MODIS Day LST` | `DayLST` | Mean daytime land-surface temperature from MODIS MOD11A2 over the script's preceding 40-day window, converted to °C. | Yes, as `DayLST` through the `lst` alias. |
| `MODIS Night LST` | `NightLST` | Mean nighttime land-surface temperature from the same MODIS window, converted to °C. It is used in the Heat Storage and Night Heat Risk calculations. | **No.** It is displayed and used analytically but is not added to `allBands` in this version. |
| `Heat Storage` | `HeatStorage` | Weighted index combining normalised day and night LST, built-density proxy, NDBI, albedo deficit and vegetation deficit. | Yes. |
| `Day Heat Risk` | `DayHeatRisk` | Composite daytime score assembled from day heat drivers, cooling deficit and environmental stress. | Yes. |
| `Night Heat Risk` | `NightHeatRisk` | Composite nighttime score combining nighttime LST, Heat Storage, built density, NDBI and cooling deficit. | Yes. |
| `Heat Class` | `HeatClass` | Four ordinal classes derived from Day Heat Risk: values 1–4 using thresholds at 0.25, 0.50 and 0.75. | Yes. |

**Distinction:** `Heat Zones` and `Heat Class` are not the same output. `Heat Zones` classifies the mean daytime LST of each grid feature using temperature thresholds in °C. `Heat Class` classifies the continuous Day Heat Risk image using score thresholds from 0 to 1.

## 4. Spectral and land-surface indicator layers

| GEE display name | Band / variable | What it represents | Included in final grid stack? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `NDVI` | `NDVI` | Normalized Difference Vegetation Index from Sentinel-2 B8 and B4; used as a vegetation-greenness indicator. | Yes. |
| `NDBI` | `NDBI` | Normalized Difference Built-up Index from Sentinel-2 B11 and B8; used as a built-up spectral proxy. | Yes. |
| `NDWI` | `NDWI` | Normalized Difference Water Index from Sentinel-2 B3 and B8; used as a water/moisture-related spectral indicator. | Yes. |
| `Albedo` | `Albedo` | Estimated surface-reflectance/albedo indicator calculated from a weighted Sentinel-2 band expression. Its formula requires validation before quantitative use. | Yes. |
| `Built Density` | `BuiltDensity` | Local mean of an NDBI > 0 binary mask within a 500 m focal radius. It is a proxy, not a mapped building-footprint density. | Yes. |
| `FVC` | `FVC` | Fractional Vegetation Cover proxy derived by scaling NDVI using `(NDVI - 0.2) / 0.6` and clamping to 0–1. | Yes. |
| `Green Cover` | `GreenCover` | Binary mask of pixels where NDVI > 0.30. The script also calculates a district-wide percentage in the console. | No. |
| `Land Cover` | `LandCover` | ESA WorldCover class image clipped to Jaipur. It is categorical land-cover information. | Yes, but see the aggregation caution in Section 8. |
| `Aerosol` | `Aerosol` | Sentinel-5P absorbing aerosol index averaged over the rolling 16-day window. It is not a particulate-matter concentration measurement. | Yes. |

## 6. Composite index and classification layers

| GEE display name | Band / variable | Interpretation in the current model | Included in final grid stack? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Cooling Capacity` | `CoolingCapacity` | Weighted indicator based on NDVI, ET, soil moisture, NDWI, FVC and LAI. It represents environmental characteristics associated with cooling potential, not a measured temperature reduction. | Yes. |
| `Vulnerability` | `Vulnerability` | Weighted combination of Day Heat Risk, Night Heat Risk and Exposure. | Yes. |
| `Vulnerability Class` | `VulnerabilityClass` | Four ordinal classes derived from the Vulnerability score. | Yes. |
| `Resilience` | `Resilience` | Weighted indicator based on NDVI, ET, soil moisture, NDWI, FVC and LAI. In this script it uses the same variables and weights as Cooling Capacity. | Yes. |
| `Resilience Class` | `ResilienceClass` | Four ordinal classes derived from the Resilience score. | Yes. |

The current formulas, weights, normalisation ranges and limitations are documented in [`methodology.md`](methodology.md). In particular, Day Heat Risk component weights sum to 1.08, and the current Resilience and Cooling Capacity calculations are not independent because they use the same inputs and weights.

## 7. Mitigation-suitability layers

These four layers are binary, rule-based suitability masks. A pixel appears in a mask when it meets the conditions coded in the script. They are not outputs of a trained AI/ML model and do not quantify the cooling effect, implementation cost or feasibility of an intervention.

| GEE display name | Implemented screening rule | Display colour | Included in final grid stack? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Cool Roof Zones` | Day Heat Risk > 0.70, NDBI > 0 and NDVI < 0.30 | Cyan | No. |
| `Urban Forest Zones` | Day Heat Risk > 0.60, NDVI < 0.30 and FVC < 0.40 | Green | No. |
| `Water Cooling Zones` | Day Heat Risk > 0.60, NDWI < 0 and soil moisture < 0.20 | Blue | No. |
| `Reflective Surface Zones` | Day Heat Risk > 0.65, albedo < 0.20 and NDBI > 0 | White | No. |

## 8. What is included in the final grid-level dataset

The script constructs `allBands` and applies `reduceRegions()` to `gridFC` with `ee.Reducer.mean()`, `scale: 1000` and `tileScale: 4`. The image stack contains **34 bands**:

| Group | Bands included in `allBands` |
|---|---|
| Thermal | `DayLST` (the `lst` alias refers to daytime LST) |
| Spectral / surface indicators | `NDVI`, `NDBI`, `NDWI`, `Albedo`, `BuiltDensity`, `FVC`, `Aerosol` |
| Meteorological / environmental | `RH`, `SurfacePressure`, `Precipitation`, `SolarRadiation`, `Evaporation`, `SoilTemp`, `ET`, `LAI`, `SoilMoisture` |
| Land / terrain / population | `LandCover`, `Elevation`, `Slope`, `Aspect`, `Population`, `PopulationExposure` |
| Composite indices / classes | `HeatStorage`, `CoolingCapacity`, `DayHeatRisk`, `NightHeatRisk`, `Exposure`, `ExposureClass`, `Vulnerability`, `VulnerabilityClass`, `Resilience`, `ResilienceClass`, `HeatClass` |

**Important output distinction:** although `NightLST` is calculated, displayed and used in the Heat Storage and Night Heat Risk equations, it is not added directly to `allBands`. The final grid-level dataset therefore does not contain a separate `NightLST` band in this script version.

## 9. Interpretation and aggregation cautions

- **One reporting grid does not make all source data 1 km resolution.** The input datasets have different native resolutions and observation characteristics. Refer to [`data_sources.md`](data_sources.md).
- **Population is currently averaged.** Because the same mean reducer is applied to every band, the grid-level Population property is not the sum of residents in each cell.
- **Categorical land cover and class bands are also passed through a mean reducer.** Their grid-level means should not be interpreted as a dominant land-cover class or a discrete class assignment. A mode reducer or other band-specific aggregation would be more appropriate for categorical outputs.
- **Several displayed layers are not part of the final stack.** These include `NightLST`, `GreenCover`, `Heat Zones` and the four mitigation masks. `AirTemp`, `DewPoint`, `WindSpeed` and `Slope` are computed but are not individually displayed; `Slope` is included in the final stack.
- **The final grid dataset is printed and displayed, not exported.** The script contains no `Export.table.toDrive()` or other export task. The `COMPLETE HEAT DATASET` layer is a map display of the `reduceRegions()` result, not proof of a saved CSV or GeoJSON file.
- **Composite scores require validation.** Their thresholds and weights are current modelling choices. See [`methodology.md`](methodology.md) for known formula and interpretation issues.

## 10. Suggested way to explore the layers in GEE

For a clear review, enable one thematic layer at a time and keep the Jaipur boundary available for spatial reference. A useful sequence is:

1. `Jaipur Boundary` and `Grid` — study area and reporting framework.
2. `MODIS Day LST` and `MODIS Night LST` — thermal patterns.
3. `NDVI`, `NDBI`, `NDWI`, `Albedo` and `Built Density` — surface characteristics.
4. `Heat Storage`, `Cooling Capacity`, `Day Heat Risk` and `Night Heat Risk` — composite analysis.
5. `Exposure`, `Vulnerability`, `Resilience` and their class layers — impact and index outputs.
6. The four mitigation-suitability layers — rule-based intervention screening.
7. `COMPLETE HEAT DATASET` — grid-level feature collection display.

The three selected screenshots in [`../assets/gee-layer-gallery/README.md`](../assets/gee-layer-gallery/README.md) provide a visual overview; this catalogue is the detailed text reference for the full set of outputs.
