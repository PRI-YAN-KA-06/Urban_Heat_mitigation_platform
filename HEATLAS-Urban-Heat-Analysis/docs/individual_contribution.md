# Individual Contribution

**Project:** HEATLAS — Urban Heat Intelligence and Mitigation Platform
**Hackathon** ISRO Bhartiya Antariksh Hackathon 2026
**Team:** Cosmic Foundry (4 member team), Rajasthan Technical University, Kota  
**Role:** Team Leader

My contribution to HEATLAS focused on research and shaping the project direction and developing its geospatial analysis/GEE component for the Jaipur pilot study.

### Key areas of contribution

- **Project framing and planning:** Helped define the problem around identifying urban heat hotspots, examining associated environmental factors, and supporting location-specific mitigation planning.
- **GEE analysis development:** Worked on the Google Earth Engine workflow for Jaipur, including the district study area, 1 km grid framework, integration of satellite and ancillary datasets, and generation of spatial analysis layers.
- **Analytical framework:** Worked on the derivation and organisation of surface and environmental indicators, day/night heat-risk components, exposure and vulnerability metrics, and rule-based mitigation-suitability layers.
- **Technical review and documentation:** Reviewed the outputs and analytical limitations, and organised the methodology, data sources, layer catalogue, implementation status, and visual evidence for reproducibility and review.
- **Team coordination and presentation:** As team leader, contributed to coordinating the project direction and communicating the proposed workflow and system architecture for the Bharatiya Antariksh Hackathon 2026 submission.

### Scope and attribution

HEATLAS was developed as a team project. The GEE analysis is the most developed technical component in the current project record. The backend remains a prototype, while the complete frontend, trained machine-learning model, intervention simulator, and AI assistant are not implemented in the supplied codebase.

The process-flow and architecture diagrams describe the broader proposed platform, including its intended expansion beyond the Jaipur pilot. They should be read as design plans rather than evidence of a fully deployed, national-scale system.
